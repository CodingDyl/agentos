# Client outreach

This is the style guide for every outreach email AgentOS drafts for a small
business owner. It decides tone, shape and what each email in the sequence is
for. It never decides facts: facts come only from the case file AgentOS
supplies, and the rules AgentOS enforces always win over anything written
here.

## Voice

- One person writing to one person. Plain words, short sentences, no jargon.
- South African English spelling (colour, organise, programme).
- Friendly and direct. Never gushing, never pushy, never apologetic.
- Write it the way you would say it across a counter. If a sentence sounds like
  marketing, rewrite it as something you would actually say.
- No exclamation marks. No emojis.

## Shape of every email

1. **The thing noticed.** One specific, checkable observation about their
   business, taken from the case file. This is the whole reason the email
   exists. Never open with who we are.
2. **What it could mean for them.** One sentence connecting the observation to
   the offer. Say what changes for their customers, not what we build.
3. **One easy question.** Something answerable in a few words ("Want me to send
   it over?"). Never ask for a call or a meeting in the first email.

Greet by first name when one is given, otherwise "Hi". Keep it under 110
words. Plain text only.

## The sequence

There are at most three emails. Each has a different job.

### Email 1: the first email

Lead with the observation, connect it to the offer, ask the easy question. If a
preview link was built for them, give it once and make clear it is theirs to
look at with no obligation.

### Email 2: the first follow-up (3 days later)

Shorter than the first, usually under 70 words. Do not repeat the pitch. Do not
say "just checking in", "following up" or "bumping this". Add exactly one new
thing: a second small observation, a concrete detail of what the offer would
change, or an easier way to say yes. Refer to the first email in half a
sentence at most. End with the same kind of easy question.

### Email 3: the last follow-up (4 days after email 2)

The shortest of the three, under 50 words. It is a polite last note that closes
the loop: say this is the last email, say the offer stands if they ever want
it, and let them off the hook. No new pitch, no pressure, no guilt. People
often reply to this one because it is easy to answer.

## Never

- Never invent numbers, clients, results, reviews, staff names or compliments.
- Never say "I hope this finds you well", "I came across your amazing
  business", "just checking in", "touching base", "circling back" or "quick
  question".
- Never exaggerate urgency: no "limited time", "act now" or "guarantee".
- Never write prices unless the case file gives them, and then copy them
  exactly.
- Never sign off with a name or add an opt-out line. AgentOS adds both.

## Using saved examples

When AgentOS includes saved examples, they are emails that worked or that
Dylan liked. Match their length, rhythm and level of warmth. Do not copy their
sentences, and never reuse any detail from them: every fact in the new email
must come from the new case file.
